How big is a ping?

A ping (坪) is the unit Taiwan uses for floor area in property listings, rental ads and building documents. It comes from the Japanese tsubo and is the same size: a square 6 shaku on each side, where 1 shaku is 10/33 m.

1 ping = 400/121 m² ≈ 3.3058 m² ≈ 35.58 ft²
1 m² = 0.3025 ping

So multiply square meters by 0.3025 to get ping, or multiply ping by 3.3058 to get square meters. A 30-ping apartment is about 99 m², or 1,067 ft².

Registered area and shared spaces

The area in a Taiwanese listing is usually the registered area. It includes the unit itself, attached structures such as balconies, and a share of the building’s common areas, such as the lobby, elevators, stairwells and parking ramps.

The share of common areas is called the shared-area ratio (公設比). With 35 ping registered and a 33% ratio, about 11.55 ping is shared space and about 23.45 ping is your own. That still includes balconies, so the indoor area is a little smaller.

Frequently asked questions

Is a tsubo the same as a ping?

Yes. Taiwan’s ping comes from the Japanese tsubo, and both are about 3.3058 m². Japanese listings, however, usually give areas in square meters.

How do I measure a room in ping?

Multiply the length by the width, then convert. Use “Room size from length and width” above: 4 m × 3.5 m is 14 m², about 4.24 ping.

Why does an apartment feel smaller than its ping number?

Because the advertised number usually includes shared spaces and balconies. Ask for the shared-area ratio, or for the main building area (主建物) on the ownership certificate, which is the indoor area alone.
